Generative and Algorithmic Design: The Brand Architecture

Communicating ideas through bold visuals, thoughtful layouts, and purposeful design..

Graphic Design Team Lead: Chioma Egboh Supervisor: Mrs. Patience Uteh
Ad

Graphic design at a professional level is a systems discipline. The BGS Graphic Design track trains students to think beyond individual assets and towards cohesive visual systems — understanding how a logo, a typeface, a colour palette, and a grid work together to produce an identity that is immediately recognisable and holds up at any scale, on any medium, in any context.

Students work with Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop at an advanced level — constructing logos on mathematical grids, managing complex multi-artboard documents, working with vector paths and producing files that meet professional print and digital specifications. Typography is taught as a technical subject: type metrics, OpenType features, optical sizing, and the logic of building a typographic system rather than picking fonts by feel.

The curriculum introduces students to generative and algorithmic design. Students experiment with Illustrator scripting, learning to think about design as a set of rules and parameters. This computational layer of thinking is increasingly valued across the design industry and distinguishes students who can adapt from those who can only replicate.

Graphic Design students leave with a portfolio that reflects both technical depth and conceptual range. Their understanding of design systems, production standards, and algorithmic methods gives them a strong foundation for further study in visual communication, interactive design, and brand strategy — along with the practical ability to begin working at a professional level without a lengthy onboarding period
