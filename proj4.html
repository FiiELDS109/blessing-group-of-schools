Zero to Shipped: The Details Nobody Notices

Solving real problems through design thinking, iteration, and human-centred creativity.

Product Design Team Lead: Goodness Onyeka Supervisor: Mrs. Audrey Nwose
Ad

Product design in the modern tech industry is a rigorous, research-driven discipline. The BGS Product Design track introduces students to the methodologies used by product teams at leading technology companies — user research, journey mapping, information architecture, interaction design, and usability testing — giving them a vocabulary and a process they can apply immediately and carry forward into any technical environment.

Students learn to work in Kittl at a beginner level and Figma at an advanced level, going beyond basic mockups to build fully interactive prototypes with realistic transitions and component states. They study interaction design principles — affordance, feedback, consistency, and error prevention — and learn to apply them in ways that make a product feel intuitive because it has been rigorously designed to be.

The track treats iteration as a technical practice, not just a creative preference. Students run structured usability tests, collect and analyse both qualitative and quantitative feedback, and use that data to make design decisions they can defend. They learn to document their rationale clearly — producing specifications and handoff files ready for production without ambiguity or back-and-forth.

Product Design students leave with a portfolio that shows not just what they built but how they think. That combination of process rigour and technical execution is what the technology industry consistently looks for in junior designers. Students from this track now aspire to pursue UX design, product management etcetera — all fields where the ability to translate human needs into functional, considered systems is the core of the work.
